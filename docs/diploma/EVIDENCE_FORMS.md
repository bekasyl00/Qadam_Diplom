### Questions

1. Tell us about the last task you gave to, or received from, an external/student team.
2. What did the initial description contain? Which missing information caused another conversation?
3. How did you agree on the deliverable, deadline, data access and acceptance conditions?
4. Who could approve a changed requirement or accept the result?
5. Which parts took the most effort? What happened when the brief was misunderstood?
6. Which information must not leave your organization or be sent to an AI provider?
7. After seeing the prototype: what would you expect the completeness score to mean?
8. Which suggested statement would you reject, and why? Would voice input help in your circumstances?

## Evaluation record schema

Use one row per actual attempt. Fields: pseudonymous participant ID, role, consent reference, task ID, condition, order, commit, provider/model, start time, end time, completion, intervention, field corrections, unsupported facts, assessor scores, latency/errors, and notes. Never include provider keys. Report denominators and abandoned attempts.

Quality rubric for each of five dimensions (clarity, completeness, verifiability, consistency, source faithfulness): **0** absent or materially incorrect; **1** partially adequate with a concrete defect; **2** adequate for the supplied scenario. Two assessors first score independently, then record disagreements and reconciliation. The app's own readiness score is not the evaluation outcome.
